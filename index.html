Simplified model for learning, not a quantitative molecular simulation. The real experiment requires a trained adult and suitable safety precautions.

Explanation

Open can: Water vapour is produced as water is heated. Vapour escapes and drives air out of the can. Fewer gas particles are shown inside while the remaining particles move faster. The pressure stays close to atmospheric pressure because the opening allows gas to escape.

Lid on: Gas cannot escape. Heating makes the particles move faster and increases pressure. Above a threshold, the lid pops off and steam escapes. Note that heating a sealed container does not make its gas particles disappear.

Cold water: The hot can is inverted into water, sealing the opening. Steam condenses and the number of gas particles falls. Air pressure outside then crushes the can.
